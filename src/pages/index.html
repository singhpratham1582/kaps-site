The problem

You are not a print manager. You have become one anyway.

The same specification, printed a year apart. Holding one against the other is the job nobody hired you to do.

  • The proof lands in your inbox and you are the one holding it against last year’s run, deciding whether the blue is the same blue.
  • You are chasing a status update on a Tuesday because the event is on Friday and nobody has called you back.
  • The delivery arrives and someone has to open a box and count. That someone is you.
  • And when it is wrong, it is not the printer who explains it to the director. It is you.

Checking your printer’s work was never supposed to be part of your job.

Ordering print has never been easier. Being able to hold somebody answerable for it has never been harder.

The portals made the transaction instant and removed the person. The cheapest local vendor kept the person and removed the process. Either way, the checking lands on your desk, and it stays there.

Why the portal cannot do this

It is built for a standard product at a standard size on standard stock, which is why it can be that cheap. What it cannot do is take responsibility. There is no one to call when the run is wrong, because there was no one on it in the first place.

Why the local vendor cannot either

There is a person, and often a good one. What there usually is not is a process. The specification lives in someone’s head, and quality holds until the person who knew your job is away. That is not a criticism of effort. It is a limit of design: a single operator cannot hold thirty-six specifications on file.
