The finishes

What the difference actually is.

Most people ordering an invitation have been shown these words and never had them explained.

Gold foil stamping

Metallic foil applied to the paper under heat and pressure. It sits on the surface with a genuine shine, which is why it catches light in a way printed gold ink never does.

Embossing

The paper is raised from behind, so the shape stands up off the page. Run a thumb across it and you feel the letter before you read it.

Letterpress

The oldest method here. The type is pressed into the paper rather than laid on top, leaving an impression you can feel from the back of the sheet. It suits thick, soft stocks.

UV spot coating

A high-gloss coating applied only to chosen areas. The contrast between the matte paper and the glossed element is the effect, more than the gloss itself.

Soft touch lamination

A velvet matte film across the whole sheet. It changes how the card feels in the hand more than how it looks.

Any of these can be combined. Some combinations work and some fight each other, and we will tell you which is which before you order rather than after.
