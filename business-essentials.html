The problem

Nobody notices business stationery. Until it is wrong.

  • The cards come back and the logo is a slightly different red than the website.
  • The letterhead runs out the week the audit starts, and the reprint does not match the box still sitting in the cupboard.
  • The brochures were fine last year. This year’s batch came off a different press at a different vendor, and now you have two versions of your own company in the same meeting room.

None of this is your job to catch. It is the sort of thing that only shows up when it has already been distributed.

Business print goes wrong for one reason: nobody wrote the specification down. Stock weight, ink values, finish, dimensions. If it lives in a conversation rather than a file, it drifts every time the order is placed by somebody new.

How it works

Specified once. Held on file.

You send what you have

You send what you have, or we create it

An existing file, a printed sample, or a description of the job. Whatever state it is in.

We check it against press requirements

Bleed, resolution, colour mode, embedded fonts. We tell you what will fail before it does.

We record the specification

Stock, weight, ink values, finish, dimensions, folds.

We print

Under colour management, to the record rather than to whatever was on the last purchase order.

We check the run against the record

Not against memory, and not against last year’s sample sitting in a drawer.

Every reprint measures against the same file

You do not re-explain it. You say how many.

Why not just order it online

Use them for the standard thing. Use us for the thing that has to match.

For 100 visiting cards on standard stock, a portal is genuinely the right answer. It is cheaper than we will be and it will be fine.

The moment it stops being the right answer is the moment the job stops being standard: a specific stock, a spot colour that has to match your brand guideline, a fold that is not in the dropdown, or a reprint next year that has to look identical to this year’s.

A portal has no record of your job and nobody to ask. That is not a flaw in the portals. It is what makes them cheap.
